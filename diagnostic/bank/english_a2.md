## EN-A2-01 — Present Simple vs Present Continuous

**EN-A2-01-Q1 — Recognition**

Tom usually ___ to school, but today his father is driving him.

A) walk  
B) walks  
C) is walking  
D) walking

**EN-A2-01-Q2 — Basic application**

Please be quiet! The students ___ an exam right now.

A) take  
B) takes  
C) are taking  
D) took

**EN-A2-01-Q3 — Independent application**

Listen! A baby ___ next door.

A) cries  
B) is crying  
C) cry  
D) cried

**EN-A2-01-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

Choose the standard sentence for the meaning 'I have this knowledge now'.

A) I am knowing the answer.  
B) I know the answer.  
C) I knows the answer.  
D) I knowing the answer.

**EN-A2-01-Q5 — Transfer / context**

Nina normally works in the office, but this week she ___ from home.

A) work  
B) works  
C) is working  
D) has work

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-A2-01</summary>

1. <u>**B) walks**</u>. 'Usually' signals routine, so Present Simple for Tom is 'walks'.

2. <u>**C) are taking**</u>. 'Right now' describes an action in progress: 'are taking'.

3. <u>**B) is crying**</u>. 'Listen!' describes a sound happening now, so Present Continuous 'is crying'.

4. <u>**B) I know the answer.**</u>. The stative verb 'know' normally uses Present Simple, not the progressive for this meaning.

5. <u>**C) is working**</u>. 'This week' contrasts a temporary arrangement with habitual office work, favoring 'is working'.

</details>

## EN-A2-03 — Future forms and intentions

**EN-A2-03-Q1 — Recognition**

Look at those black storm clouds! It ___ soon.

A) is going to rain  
B) rained  
C) rains yesterday  
D) to rain

**EN-A2-03-Q2 — Basic application**

The phone is ringing. I ___ it!

A) answered  
B) am answering yesterday  
C) will answer  
D) answering

**EN-A2-03-Q3 — Independent application**

They have decided to buy a new bicycle next month. They ___ one.

A) buying  
B) are going to buy  
C) bought  
D) have buy

**EN-A2-03-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

I promise I ___ you the notes tomorrow.

A) will send  
B) sent  
C) am send  
D) was sending

**EN-A2-03-Q5 — Transfer / context**

If you press this button, the screen ___ on.

A) will turn  
B) turned  
C) turning  
D) have turned

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-A2-03</summary>

1. <u>**A) is going to rain**</u>. Current visible evidence supports the prediction 'is going to rain'.

2. <u>**C) will answer**</u>. A spontaneous decision made now is naturally expressed with 'will answer'.

3. <u>**B) are going to buy**</u>. A prior intention or plan takes 'are going to + base verb'.

4. <u>**A) will send**</u>. Promises commonly use 'will + base verb': 'I will send'.

5. <u>**A) will turn**</u>. First conditional result: if + present, 'will + base verb' for the expected future effect.

</details>

## EN-A2-04 — Quantifiers, comparatives and superlatives

**EN-A2-04-Q1 — Recognition**

How ___ apples do you need?

A) much  
B) many  
C) little  
D) any of

**EN-A2-04-Q2 — Basic application**

There is very ___ milk left; we need to buy more.

A) few  
B) many  
C) little  
D) a few

**EN-A2-04-Q3 — Independent application**

This room is ___ than that one.

A) biggest  
B) bigger  
C) most big  
D) as biger

**EN-A2-04-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

Of all the runners, Mina was the ___.

A) faster  
B) fast  
C) fastest  
D) most fast

**EN-A2-04-Q5 — Transfer / context**

This year there are ___ students in the class than last year.

A) less  
B) fewer  
C) little  
D) much

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-A2-04</summary>

1. <u>**B) many**</u>. 'Apples' is a countable plural; use 'many' in a quantity question.

2. <u>**C) little**</u>. 'Milk' is uncountable; 'little' means not much. 'Few' applies to countable nouns.

3. <u>**B) bigger**</u>. Comparison between two uses comparative adjective 'bigger ... than'.

4. <u>**C) fastest**</u>. Superlative among all runners is 'the fastest'.

5. <u>**B) fewer**</u>. 'Students' is countable plural, so 'fewer' compares numbers; 'less' normally compares uncountable amounts.

</details>

## EN-A2-05 — Modals and linking clauses

**EN-A2-05-Q1 — Recognition**

At a red traffic light, drivers ___ stop.

A) may  
B) must  
C) might  
D) can

**EN-A2-05-Q2 — Basic application**

You look exhausted. You ___ get some rest.

A) should  
B) can to  
C) mustn't  
D) may not

**EN-A2-05-Q3 — Independent application**

When she was four years old, she ___ already count to twenty.

A) can  
B) could  
C) can to  
D) was can

**EN-A2-05-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

If it ___ tomorrow, we will stay at home.

A) will rain  
B) rained  
C) rains  
D) raining

**EN-A2-05-Q5 — Transfer / context**

We stayed inside ___ it was raining heavily.

A) although  
B) because  
C) unless  
D) but

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-A2-05</summary>

1. <u>**B) must**</u>. 'Must' expresses a strong obligation: drivers must stop.

2. <u>**A) should**</u>. 'Should' expresses advice. The other options are ungrammatical or mean prohibition/possible non-action.

3. <u>**B) could**</u>. Past ability is expressed with 'could + base verb'.

4. <u>**C) rains**</u>. First conditional uses Present Simple in the if-clause: 'If it rains, we will ...'.

5. <u>**B) because**</u>. 'Because' gives the reason for staying indoors. The other conjunctions express contrast or conditions.

</details>
