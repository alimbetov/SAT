## EN-B2-01 — Perfect aspect and narrative sequence

**EN-B2-01-Q1 — Recognition**

By the time I arrived at the station, the train ___.

A) leaves  
B) has left  
C) had left  
D) will leave

**EN-B2-01-Q2 — Basic application**

By next Friday, she ___ the entire project.

A) will have completed  
B) has completed yesterday  
C) had completed  
D) completing

**EN-B2-01-Q3 — Independent application**

They ___ for two hours when the bus finally arrived.

A) are waiting  
B) had been waiting  
C) have waited tomorrow  
D) will be waiting

**EN-B2-01-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

By the time she graduated in 2020, she ___ two internships.

A) completes  
B) will complete  
C) had completed  
D) is completing

**EN-B2-01-Q5 — Transfer / context**

When I got home last night, I realized I ___ my keys at the office.

A) had left  
B) leave  
C) will leave  
D) am leaving

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-B2-01</summary>

1. <u>**C) had left**</u>. The departure happened before another past event (my arrival), so Past Perfect 'had left' marks the earlier event.

2. <u>**A) will have completed**</u>. 'By next Friday' marks a future deadline, so Future Perfect 'will have completed' expresses completion by then.

3. <u>**B) had been waiting**</u>. Past Perfect Continuous emphasizes the duration leading up to the later past event: 'had been waiting'.

4. <u>**C) had completed**</u>. Completion before another past reference point (graduation in 2020) takes Past Perfect 'had completed'.

5. <u>**A) had left**</u>. Leaving the keys happened before the past realization: 'had left'.

</details>

## EN-B2-02 — Third and mixed conditionals

**EN-B2-02-Q1 — Recognition**

If she had studied harder, she ___ the exam last week.

A) would pass  
B) will have passed  
C) would have passed  
D) had passed

**EN-B2-02-Q2 — Basic application**

If I had known about the delay yesterday, I ___ you immediately.

A) will call  
B) would call  
C) would have called  
D) had called

**EN-B2-02-Q3 — Independent application**

If I had gone to bed earlier last night, I ___ so tired now.

A) wouldn't be  
B) wouldn't have been yesterday  
C) won't be yesterday  
D) am not

**EN-B2-02-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

If he were more careful generally, he ___ that vase yesterday.

A) won't break  
B) wouldn't have broken  
C) hadn't broken  
D) doesn't break

**EN-B2-02-Q5 — Transfer / context**

If they had studied English for years, they ___ this article easily now.

A) would have understood yesterday  
B) will understand yesterday  
C) would understand  
D) had understood

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-B2-02</summary>

1. <u>**C) would have passed**</u>. Unreal past condition: If + Past Perfect, would have + past participle.

2. <u>**C) would have called**</u>. Counterfactual past result uses 'would have called' after 'If I had known'.

3. <u>**A) wouldn't be**</u>. Mixed conditional connects an unreal past cause to a present result: would not be tired now.

4. <u>**B) wouldn't have broken**</u>. Present unreal trait with a past consequence: If he were more careful, he wouldn't have broken it yesterday.

5. <u>**C) would understand**</u>. Unreal past learning history implies unreal present ability: 'would understand ... now'.

</details>

## EN-B2-03 — Advanced passive and causative

**EN-B2-03-Q1 — Recognition**

I ___ at a salon yesterday; a professional did it for me.

A) cutting my hair  
B) had my hair cut  
C) have cut my hair tomorrow  
D) was my hair cutting

**EN-B2-03-Q2 — Basic application**

The report ___ by the committee yesterday afternoon.

A) was approved  
B) has approve  
C) is approving  
D) was approve

**EN-B2-03-Q3 — Independent application**

Last week, Clara ___ by a mechanic after her car broke down.

A) got her car repaired  
B) got her car repair  
C) had repaired her car by herself  
D) was got her car repair

**EN-B2-03-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

He was made ___ publicly for his mistake.

A) apologize  
B) apologizing  
C) to apologize  
D) apologized

**EN-B2-03-Q5 — Transfer / context**

The old bridge ___ right now, so drivers must use the detour.

A) repairs  
B) is being repaired  
C) has been repair  
D) was repairing

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-B2-03</summary>

1. <u>**B) had my hair cut**</u>. Causative 'have + object + past participle' means someone else did the service: 'had my hair cut'.

2. <u>**A) was approved**</u>. Past passive is 'was + past participle': was approved.

3. <u>**A) got her car repaired**</u>. Causative 'got + object + past participle' means a mechanic repaired her car for her.

4. <u>**C) to apologize**</u>. Passive of 'make somebody do something' becomes 'somebody was made to do something'.

5. <u>**B) is being repaired**</u>. A passive action happening now uses Present Continuous passive 'is being repaired'.

</details>

## EN-B2-05 — Complex clauses, inversion and emphasis

**EN-B2-05-Q1 — Recognition**

___ it was raining heavily, the team continued playing.

A) Although  
B) Despite  
C) In spite  
D) However

**EN-B2-05-Q2 — Basic application**

___ the heavy rain, the match continued.

A) Although  
B) Even though  
C) Despite  
D) Whereas

**EN-B2-05-Q3 — Independent application**

Not only ___ finish early, but he also helped his classmates.

A) he did  
B) did he  
C) he has  
D) he does

**EN-B2-05-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

Hardly ___ we arrived when the lights went out.

A) had  
B) have  
C) will  
D) are

**EN-B2-05-Q5 — Transfer / context**

It was Maria ___ solved the puzzle, not the other students.

A) whom  
B) whose  
C) who  
D) which

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — EN-B2-05</summary>

1. <u>**A) Although**</u>. 'Although' introduces a complete subordinate clause; 'despite' and 'in spite of' require a noun phrase or -ing, and 'however' needs independent-clause punctuation.

2. <u>**C) Despite**</u>. 'Despite' directly precedes the noun phrase 'the heavy rain'; 'although/even though' require a clause.

3. <u>**B) did he**</u>. Fronted negative/restrictive adverbial 'Not only' causes subject–auxiliary inversion: 'did he finish'.

4. <u>**A) had**</u>. The inversion pattern 'Hardly had + subject + past participle ... when ...' expresses quick sequence in the past.

5. <u>**C) who**</u>. Cleft sentence highlights the person who performed the action: 'It was Maria who solved...'.

</details>
