## PSD-01 — Ratios, rates and units

**PSD-01-Q1**

A basket contains 3 red apples and 5 green apples. What is the ratio of red to green apples?

A) 3:5  
B) 5:3  
C) 3:8  
D) 8:5

**PSD-01-Q2**

A car travels 240 kilometers in 4 hours at a constant speed. What is its speed?

A) 40 km/h  
B) 50 km/h  
C) 60 km/h  
D) 960 km/h

**PSD-01-Q3**

Paint is mixed with blue and white in the ratio 2:3. If blue paint is 8 liters, how much white paint is needed?

A) 3 liters  
B) 8 liters  
C) 12 liters  
D) 24 liters

**PSD-01-Q4**

How many meters are in 2.5 kilometers?

A) 25  
B) 250  
C) 2500  
D) 25000

**PSD-01-Q5**

A recipe uses 2 cups of rice for 3 servings. How many cups are needed for 9 servings at the same ratio?

A) 3  
B) 4  
C) 6  
D) 9

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-01</summary>

1. <u>**A) 3:5**</u>. Red-to-green is 3:5; 3:8 compares red with all apples instead.

2. <u>**C) 60 km/h**</u>. Speed = distance/time =240/4=60 kilometers per hour.

3. <u>**C) 12 liters**</u>. Blue 2 parts correspond to 8 L, so each part is 4 L; white 3 parts needs 12 L.

4. <u>**C) 2500**</u>. One kilometer equals 1000 meters; 2.5·1000=2500.

5. <u>**C) 6**</u>. Nine servings are triple three servings, so rice is 2·3=6 cups.

</details>

## PSD-02 — Percentages and percent change

**PSD-02-Q1**

What is 25% of 80?

A) 10  
B) 20  
C) 25  
D) 40

**PSD-02-Q2**

Increase 50 by 10%. What is the new value?

A) 51  
B) 55  
C) 60  
D) 65

**PSD-02-Q3**

A $120 jacket is discounted by 25%. What is the sale price?

A) $30  
B) $75  
C) $90  
D) $95

**PSD-02-Q4**

A price rises 20% and then falls 20%. Starting from $100, what is the final price?

A) $80  
B) $96  
C) $100  
D) $104

**PSD-02-Q5**

An item costs $45 after a 10% discount. What was the original price?

A) $40.50  
B) $49.50  
C) $50  
D) $55

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-02</summary>

1. <u>**B) 20**</u>. 25%=1/4; one quarter of 80 is 20.

2. <u>**B) 55**</u>. 10% of 50 is 5, so 50+5=55.

3. <u>**C) $90**</u>. Discount is 120·0.25=30; remaining price 120−30=90.

4. <u>**B) $96**</u>. After growth: 100·1.2=120. Decrease applies to 120: 120·0.8=96, not 100.

5. <u>**C) $50**</u>. $45 represents 90% of original P, so 0.9P=45 and P=50.

</details>

## PSD-03 — One-variable statistics

**PSD-03-Q1**

What is the median of the values 1, 3, 5?

A) 1  
B) 3  
C) 4  
D) 5

**PSD-03-Q2**

Find the mean of 2, 4, 8, 10.

A) 4  
B) 5  
C) 6  
D) 8

**PSD-03-Q3**

Find the range of 4, 7, 10, 14.

A) 7  
B) 10  
C) 11  
D) 14

**PSD-03-Q4**

Which summary is generally more resistant to a single extremely large outlier?

A) Mean  
B) Median  
C) Maximum  
D) Range

**PSD-03-Q5**

Three students scored 10 points each and two students scored 20 points each. What is their mean score?

A) 12  
B) 14  
C) 15  
D) 16

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-03</summary>

1. <u>**B) 3**</u>. With three ordered values, median is the middle value, 3.

2. <u>**C) 6**</u>. Mean=(2+4+8+10)/4=24/4=6.

3. <u>**B) 10**</u>. Range=max−min=14−4=10. This summary measures the distance between the smallest and largest observed values.

4. <u>**B) Median**</u>. The median depends on order and is usually less sensitive to extreme values than the mean.

5. <u>**B) 14**</u>. Total is 3·10+2·20=70 points across 5 students, giving mean 14.

</details>

## PSD-04 — Two-variable data and scatterplots

**PSD-04-Q1**

In a scatterplot, points tend to rise from left to right. What association does this suggest?

A) Positive  
B) Negative  
C) No association  
D) A constant value for all points

**PSD-04-Q2**

A model for y is y=2x+3. What is predicted at x=4?

A) 8  
B) 9  
C) 11  
D) 14

**PSD-04-Q3**

A line of best fit passes through (1,2) and (3,6). What is its slope?

A) 1  
B) 2  
C) 3  
D) 4

**PSD-04-Q4**

A city observes that ice cream sales and swimming accidents both increase in summer. What conclusion is justified by this association alone?

A) Ice cream causes accidents  
B) Accidents cause ice cream sales  
C) Both variables may respond to warmer weather  
D) The two variables must be independent

**PSD-04-Q5**

A fitted line is y=5+1.5x, where x is hours practiced and y is expected points. What is predicted for 10 hours?

A) 15  
B) 17.5  
C) 20  
D) 25

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-04</summary>

1. <u>**A) Positive**</u>. As x grows, y generally grows: positive association.

2. <u>**C) 11**</u>. Substitute 4: 2·4+3=11. The line predicts this value; a real observation may differ from the fitted model.

3. <u>**B) 2**</u>. Rise/run=(6−2)/(3−1)=4/2=2.

4. <u>**C) Both variables may respond to warmer weather**</u>. An association does not prove causation; season/temperature could influence both.

5. <u>**C) 20**</u>. Model prediction is 5+1.5·10=20 points. This is a prediction, not guaranteed outcome.

</details>

## PSD-05 — Probability and conditional probability

**PSD-05-Q1**

A fair spinner has 4 equal sectors, one colored red. What is P(red)?

A) 1/8  
B) 1/4  
C) 1/2  
D) 3/4

**PSD-05-Q2**

What is the probability of rolling an even number on a fair six-sided die?

A) 1/6  
B) 1/3  
C) 1/2  
D) 2/3

**PSD-05-Q3**

A bag has 3 blue and 2 red marbles. One is chosen at random. P(blue)=?

A) 2/5  
B) 3/5  
C) 3/2  
D) 2/3

**PSD-05-Q4**

Of 5 students who play sports, 3 wear glasses. If a sports player is selected at random, P(glasses | sports)=?

A) 3/8  
B) 3/5  
C) 5/8  
D) 5/3

**PSD-05-Q5**

A fair coin is tossed twice independently. What is the probability of two heads?

A) 1/2  
B) 1/3  
C) 1/4  
D) 3/4

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-05</summary>

1. <u>**B) 1/4**</u>. One favorable sector among four equally likely outcomes: 1/4.

2. <u>**C) 1/2**</u>. Even outcomes 2,4,6: 3 of 6, or 1/2.

3. <u>**B) 3/5**</u>. There are 3 favorable blue marbles among 5 total, P=3/5.

4. <u>**B) 3/5**</u>. Conditioning restricts the denominator to the 5 sports players; 3 have glasses, so 3/5.

5. <u>**C) 1/4**</u>. Each head is 1/2 and independent: (1/2)(1/2)=1/4.

</details>

## PSD-06 — Sampling and margin of error

**PSD-06-Q1**

Which sampling method gives each individual in a population an equal chance of selection?

A) Convenience sample  
B) Simple random sample  
C) Voluntary-response sample  
D) Choosing only top students

**PSD-06-Q2**

All else equal, how does a larger well-designed random sample usually affect sampling margin of error?

A) It increases  
B) It tends to decrease  
C) It always becomes zero  
D) It becomes the sample mean

**PSD-06-Q3**

A poll estimates 60% support with a margin of error ±4 percentage points. Which interval matches the estimate?

A) 4%–60%  
B) 56%–64%  
C) 60%–64%  
D) 64%–68%

**PSD-06-Q4**

A school surveys only members of its chess club about all students' after-school interests. What is the main concern?

A) The sample may not represent all students  
B) It is always a perfect random sample  
C) The sample is too large  
D) No question can be asked about interests

**PSD-06-Q5**

A poll estimates support at 52% with margin of error ±5 percentage points. Does this alone establish that true support is above 50%?

A) Yes, because 52% > 50%  
B) Yes, because sample sizes never matter  
C) No, because the interval includes values below 50%  
D) No, because support must equal 47%

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-06</summary>

1. <u>**B) Simple random sample**</u>. A simple random sample gives each individual equal selection probability; convenience selection does not.

2. <u>**B) It tends to decrease**</u>. For the same confidence level and design, increased sample size generally reduces sampling variation and margin of error.

3. <u>**B) 56%–64%**</u>. Subtract/add 4 percentage points from 60%, yielding 56%–64%.

4. <u>**A) The sample may not represent all students**</u>. Club members may differ systematically from the whole school, creating selection bias.

5. <u>**C) No, because the interval includes values below 50%**</u>. The interval 47%–57% crosses 50%, so majority support is not established by this interval alone.

</details>

## PSD-07 — Observational studies and experiments

**PSD-07-Q1**

Which study design most directly supports a causal claim about an intervention when well executed?

A) An observational survey  
B) A randomized controlled experiment  
C) A voluntary online poll  
D) A simple correlation graph

**PSD-07-Q2**

Which feature most directly helps a sample generalize to the population it represents?

A) Random sampling from the target population  
B) Assigning all participants the same treatment  
C) A colorful graph  
D) Removing all low results

**PSD-07-Q3**

A study finds an association between extra tutoring and higher grades. Without controlling for other factors, what can be concluded?

A) Tutoring always causes higher grades  
B) Higher grades cause tutoring  
C) There is an association; causation is not established  
D) The variables are unrelated

**PSD-07-Q4**

Why might both sunscreen sales and ice cream sales rise on hot days?

A) Buying sunscreen causes ice cream sales  
B) Temperature is a possible confounding factor  
C) The data must be incorrect  
D) No variable influences either outcome

**PSD-07-Q5**

A school randomly assigns 60 students to either a new math course or a standard course, then compares later test scores. What is the main purpose of random assignment?

A) Make the groups comparable on average before treatment  
B) Ensure everyone scores 100%  
C) Guarantee a nationally representative sample  
D) Remove the need to measure scores

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — PSD-07</summary>

1. <u>**B) A randomized controlled experiment**</u>. Random assignment balances many confounders on average and strengthens causal inference.

2. <u>**A) Random sampling from the target population**</u>. Random sampling helps represent a defined population; assignment concerns treatment effects.

3. <u>**C) There is an association; causation is not established**</u>. Students may differ in motivation, resources, or prior grades; observational association alone is insufficient.

4. <u>**B) Temperature is a possible confounding factor**</u>. Hot weather can influence both behaviors, making temperature a lurking/confounding variable.

5. <u>**A) Make the groups comparable on average before treatment**</u>. Random assignment supports causal comparison by reducing systematic baseline differences, but does not guarantee perfect balance or external representativeness.

</details>
