## ADV-01 — Equivalent expressions

**ADV-01-Q1 — Recognition**

Which expression is equivalent to 3(x + 4)?

A) 3x + 4  
B) 3x + 12  
C) x + 12  
D) 12x

**ADV-01-Q2 — Basic application**

Expand (x + 2)(x + 3).

A) x² + 6  
B) x² + 5x + 6  
C) x² + 6x + 5  
D) 2x + 5

**ADV-01-Q3 — Independent application**

Simplify 2x + 4x − 5.

A) 6x − 5  
B) 6x + 5  
C) 8x − 5  
D) 6x

**ADV-01-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

Simplify −(2x − 3) + x.

A) 3x − 3  
B) −3x + 3  
C) −x + 3  
D) −x − 3

**ADV-01-Q5 — Transfer / context**

A rectangle has length 7 and width x. Which expression represents its perimeter?

A) 7x  
B) 14 + 2x  
C) 7 + x  
D) 14x

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-01</summary>

1. <u>**B) 3x + 12**</u>. Distribute 3 to both terms: 3x + 3·4 = 3x+12.

2. <u>**B) x² + 5x + 6**</u>. Multiply all terms: x²+3x+2x+6 = x²+5x+6.

3. <u>**A) 6x − 5**</u>. Combine only like x terms: 2x+4x=6x; constant remains −5.

4. <u>**C) −x + 3**</u>. Negative sign distributes to both terms: −2x+3+x=−x+3.

5. <u>**B) 14 + 2x**</u>. Perimeter = 2(length+width)=2(7+x)=14+2x.

</details>

## ADV-02 — Quadratic equations and functions

**ADV-02-Q1 — Recognition**

What are all real solutions of x² = 9?

A) 3 only  
B) −3 only  
C) −3 and 3  
D) 9 only

**ADV-02-Q2 — Basic application**

Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.

A) −2, −3  
B) 1, 6  
C) 2, 3  
D) −1, −6

**ADV-02-Q3 — Independent application**

What is the vertex of y = (x − 4)² + 1?

A) (−4,1)  
B) (4,1)  
C) (1,4)  
D) (4,−1)

**ADV-02-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

How many real roots does x² + 2x + 5 = 0 have?

A) 0  
B) 1  
C) 2  
D) Infinitely many

**ADV-02-Q5 — Transfer / context**

A toy's height is h(t) = −5t² + 20t + 1, for t≥0. At what time does it reach maximum height?

A) 1 second  
B) 2 seconds  
C) 4 seconds  
D) 5 seconds

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-02</summary>

1. <u>**C) −3 and 3**</u>. Both 3² and (−3)² equal 9. The square-root equation has two solutions.

2. <u>**C) 2, 3**</u>. Factor (x−2)(x−3)=0; roots are 2 and 3.

3. <u>**B) (4,1)**</u>. Vertex form y=(x−h)²+k has vertex (h,k)=(4,1).

4. <u>**A) 0**</u>. Discriminant b²−4ac=4−20=−16<0: no real roots.

5. <u>**B) 2 seconds**</u>. For a downward quadratic, peak is at t=−b/(2a)=−20/(−10)=2.

</details>

## ADV-03 — Polynomial functions

**ADV-03-Q1 — Recognition**

What is the degree of p(x) = 3x⁴ − 2x + 1?

A) 1  
B) 2  
C) 3  
D) 4

**ADV-03-Q2 — Basic application**

If (x − 2) is a factor of p(x), what must be true?

A) p(0)=2  
B) p(2)=0  
C) p(−2)=0  
D) p(2)=2

**ADV-03-Q3 — Independent application**

Evaluate p(2) for p(x)=x³−4x.

A) −8  
B) 0  
C) 4  
D) 8

**ADV-03-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

For p(x)=−2x³+x, what happens to p(x) as x approaches positive infinity?

A) It approaches positive infinity  
B) It approaches negative infinity  
C) It approaches 0  
D) It stays equal to −2

**ADV-03-Q5 — Transfer / context**

Revenue from x items is R(x)=x(20−x). What is R(4)?

A) 16  
B) 40  
C) 64  
D) 80

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-03</summary>

1. <u>**D) 4**</u>. The highest exponent with nonzero coefficient is 4.

2. <u>**B) p(2)=0**</u>. Factor theorem: x−2 divides p(x) exactly if p(2)=0.

3. <u>**B) 0**</u>. p(2)=2³−4·2=8−8=0. Evaluate the cubic term and the multiplication before subtracting.

4. <u>**B) It approaches negative infinity**</u>. Leading term −2x³ dominates for large positive x, so p(x) becomes very negative.

5. <u>**C) 64**</u>. Substitute x=4: R(4)=4(20−4)=4·16=64.

</details>

## ADV-04 — Exponential functions

**ADV-04-Q1 — Recognition**

For f(x)=3·2ˣ, what is f(2)?

A) 6  
B) 9  
C) 12  
D) 24

**ADV-04-Q2 — Basic application**

A $100 investment grows by 5% each year. Which model gives its value after t years?

A) 100 + 5t  
B) 100(0.95)ᵗ  
C) 100(1.05)ᵗ  
D) 5(100)ᵗ

**ADV-04-Q3 — Independent application**

Solve 2ˣ = 16.

A) 2  
B) 3  
C) 4  
D) 8

**ADV-04-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

A quantity starts at 80 and halves every hour. How much remains after 3 hours?

A) 5  
B) 10  
C) 20  
D) 40

**ADV-04-Q5 — Transfer / context**

A bacteria culture starts with 7 cells and doubles each day. How many cells are there after 3 days?

A) 21  
B) 28  
C) 42  
D) 56

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-04</summary>

1. <u>**C) 12**</u>. f(2)=3·2²=12. The exponent applies to base 2, while the coefficient 3 multiplies the result.

2. <u>**C) 100(1.05)ᵗ**</u>. Each year multiplies the current amount by 1.05, hence repeated compound growth.

3. <u>**C) 4**</u>. 16=2⁴, so x=4. With the same positive base, equal powers require equal exponents.

4. <u>**B) 10**</u>. After three halvings: 80·(1/2)³=80/8=10.

5. <u>**D) 56**</u>. Three doublings give 7·2³=56.

</details>

## ADV-05 — Rational expressions and equations

**ADV-05-Q1 — Recognition**

For which real x is x/(x−2) undefined?

A) −2  
B) 0  
C) 1  
D) 2

**ADV-05-Q2 — Basic application**

For x≠3, simplify (x²−9)/(x−3).

A) x−3  
B) x+3  
C) x²+3  
D) 1

**ADV-05-Q3 — Independent application**

Solve 1/x = 1/4.

A) −4  
B) 1/4  
C) 4  
D) 0

**ADV-05-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

Solve (x+1)/(x−1)=2.

A) 1  
B) 2  
C) 3  
D) −3

**ADV-05-Q5 — Transfer / context**

One machine completes a task in t hours. If two identical machines work at the same constant rate simultaneously, how long do they take together?

A) 2t hours  
B) t hours  
C) t/2 hours  
D) t² hours

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-05</summary>

1. <u>**D) 2**</u>. The denominator is zero at x=2, so that value is excluded.

2. <u>**B) x+3**</u>. Factor x²−9=(x−3)(x+3), cancel x−3 only where x≠3.

3. <u>**C) 4**</u>. Cross-multiply or take reciprocals (with x≠0): x=4.

4. <u>**C) 3**</u>. Multiply by x−1 (x≠1): x+1=2x−2 ⇒ x=3; x=3 is allowed.

5. <u>**C) t/2 hours**</u>. Each machine's rate is 1/t tasks per hour. Combined rate is 2/t, so time is t/2.

</details>

## ADV-06 — Radicals and radical equations

**ADV-06-Q1 — Recognition**

What is the principal square root of 49?

A) −7  
B) 7  
C) ±7  
D) 14

**ADV-06-Q2 — Basic application**

Solve √(x+1) = 4.

A) 3  
B) 8  
C) 15  
D) 17

**ADV-06-Q3 — Independent application**

Solve √(3x+1)=5.

A) 6  
B) 8  
C) 9  
D) 12

**ADV-06-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

Which expression equals √(x²) for every real x?

A) x  
B) −x  
C) |x|  
D) x²

**ADV-06-Q5 — Transfer / context**

A square has area 72 square units. What is its exact positive side length?

A) 6√2  
B) 12√2  
C) 36  
D) 72

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-06</summary>

1. <u>**B) 7**</u>. The symbol √49 denotes the nonnegative (principal) square root: 7.

2. <u>**C) 15**</u>. Square both sides: x+1=16, so x=15; substitution gives √16=4.

3. <u>**B) 8**</u>. Square: 3x+1=25 ⇒ 3x=24 ⇒ x=8; the radicand is 25.

4. <u>**C) |x|**</u>. Principal square root is nonnegative; √(x²)=|x|, not always x when x<0.

5. <u>**A) 6√2**</u>. Side length s satisfies s²=72; s=√72=√(36·2)=6√2.

</details>

## ADV-07 — Absolute value

**ADV-07-Q1 — Recognition**

What is |−7|?

A) −7  
B) 0  
C) 7  
D) 14

**ADV-07-Q2 — Basic application**

What are all real solutions of |x|=5?

A) 5 only  
B) −5 only  
C) −5 and 5  
D) 0 and 5

**ADV-07-Q3 — Independent application**

Solve |x−2|=3.

A) −1 and 5  
B) 1 and 5  
C) −3 and 3  
D) 2 and 3

**ADV-07-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

How many real solutions does |x+1|=−2 have?

A) 0  
B) 1  
C) 2  
D) Infinitely many

**ADV-07-Q5 — Transfer / context**

On a number line, which points are 7 units away from 4?

A) −7 and 7  
B) −3 and 11  
C) 3 and 11  
D) −11 and 3

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-07</summary>

1. <u>**C) 7**</u>. Absolute value is distance from zero, so |−7|=7.

2. <u>**C) −5 and 5**</u>. Distance 5 from zero corresponds to x=−5 and x=5.

3. <u>**A) −1 and 5**</u>. Either x−2=3 giving x=5 or x−2=−3 giving x=−1.

4. <u>**A) 0**</u>. An absolute value is always ≥0, so cannot equal −2.

5. <u>**B) −3 and 11**</u>. Distance equation |x−4|=7 gives x=4±7, hence −3 and 11.

</details>

## ADV-08 — Nonlinear functions

**ADV-08-Q1 — Recognition**

What kind of graph does y=x² have?

A) Straight line  
B) Parabola  
C) Circle  
D) Horizontal line

**ADV-08-Q2 — Basic application**

For f(x)=x²+3, find f(−2).

A) −1  
B) 1  
C) 5  
D) 7

**ADV-08-Q3 — Independent application**

Which function is nonlinear?

A) y=4x−2  
B) y=5  
C) y=x³  
D) y=−x

**ADV-08-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

What are the x-intercepts of y=x²−4?

A) −4 and 4  
B) −2 and 2  
C) 0 and 4  
D) 2 only

**ADV-08-Q5 — Transfer / context**

A square's side is (x+1) cm. When x=3, what is its area?

A) 4 cm²  
B) 8 cm²  
C) 12 cm²  
D) 16 cm²

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-08</summary>

1. <u>**B) Parabola**</u>. A quadratic function has a parabolic graph; it is not linear.

2. <u>**D) 7**</u>. Square −2 first: (−2)²=4; add 3 to get 7.

3. <u>**C) y=x³**</u>. The cubic x³ does not have constant slope; the others are linear (including the constant function).

4. <u>**B) −2 and 2**</u>. Set y=0: x²=4, so x=±2. The corresponding intercepts are (−2,0) and (2,0).

5. <u>**D) 16 cm²**</u>. Side is 3+1=4 cm; area is (x+1)²=16 cm².

</details>

## ADV-09 — Nonlinear equations and systems

**ADV-09-Q1 — Recognition**

For y=x² and y=4, what x-values occur at intersections?

A) 0 only  
B) 2 only  
C) −2 and 2  
D) −4 and 4

**ADV-09-Q2 — Basic application**

Which intersection points solve y=x² and y=x+2?

A) (−1,1) and (2,4)  
B) (1,1) and (2,4)  
C) (−2,4) and (1,1)  
D) (0,0) and (2,4)

**ADV-09-Q3 — Independent application**

How many real intersections do y=x² and y=−1 have?

A) 0  
B) 1  
C) 2  
D) Infinitely many

**ADV-09-Q4 — Misconception / trap**

How many intersections do y=x² and y=2x−1 have?

A) 0  
B) 1  
C) 2  
D) 3

**ADV-09-Q5 — Transfer / context**

The system y=2x+3 and y=x² has which x-coordinates of intersections?

A) −3 and 1  
B) −1 and 3  
C) 1 and 3  
D) −3 and −1

<details><summary>Teacher-only answer key and explanations — ADV-09</summary>

1. <u>**C) −2 and 2**</u>. Set x²=4: x=−2 or 2; both have y=4.

2. <u>**A) (−1,1) and (2,4)**</u>. Equate: x²=x+2 ⇒ (x−2)(x+1)=0; x=−1 gives y=1, x=2 gives y=4.

3. <u>**A) 0**</u>. Squares of real numbers are nonnegative, so cannot equal −1.

4. <u>**B) 1**</u>. Equation x²=2x−1 ⇒ (x−1)²=0, a repeated root; the line is tangent.

5. <u>**B) −1 and 3**</u>. Set x²=2x+3 ⇒ x²−2x−3=(x−3)(x+1)=0; x=3 or x=−1.

</details>
